There is one story about him that I have told more times than I can count. I told it at parties, at dinners and to people who never met him. It always got a laugh, and I always laughed with them.

I don't laugh at it the same way anymore. But it is still the memory I reach for first, because it is the one where he is most alive.

## The night on the road

He was drunk. Properly drunk, on god knows what, lying in the middle of the road.

We were trying to get him to the hospital when the cops found us. Somehow, we convinced them to let us take him. So we all got into my car, and a cop drove it.

That was the longest ride of my life. A cop at the wheel of my car, him passed out in the back, and the rest of us sitting dead silent, not daring to look at each other. Nobody said a word. Every time someone made eye contact, we had to look out of the window so we wouldn't burst out laughing.

The doctors said it was alcohol poisoning, and they admitted him. We sat in the visitors' area, half asleep, until he came back to his senses.

At 5am we took him home. We talked the cops out of filing anything or calling his family, and as far as his parents ever knew, it was just another late night out with us.

## The calmest of us all

However stupid he could be, and he could be very stupid, he was the calmest person in every room. Nothing seemed to shake him. He never complained, not about plans falling apart, not about where we ended up, not about anything. Whatever happened, he just adjusted and made it work.

He always had the best ideas. We shot most of them down. We laughed at them, argued with them, and told him they would never work. Looking back, most of them were simply ahead of their time. He saw things a few years before the rest of us did, and he never once held it against us that we couldn't see them yet.

I wish I had told him that. I wish I had said, even once, "You were right."

## Not one photo

I knew him for almost sixteen years. In all that time, we never took a single photo together. Not one. None of us in our group have one with him.

It never crossed our minds. You don't take photos of something you think will always be there. There was always a next time. Another trip, another night out, another plan. There was always a next time, until there wasn't.

Now I would give almost anything for one blurry, badly lit picture of the two of us. Not a good one. Just one that proves we stood in the same place at the same time and didn't think twice about it.

## The silence

It has been almost a year since he passed away.

For most of that year, I didn't talk about him. When someone said his name, I went quiet. I changed the subject, or nodded and waited for the moment to pass. I think I believed that if I didn't say it out loud, it wouldn't be quite as true.

Last month, something shifted. I started talking about him. To friends, to family, sometimes to people who barely knew him. And something strange happened: the more I talked, the closer he felt, not further.

There is a line I keep coming back to:

> We are meant to lose the people we love. How else would we know how important they are to us?

I hate how true it is. I didn't know how much of my life had his fingerprints on it until there was no one left to leave new ones.

## Everything he left me

He is everywhere, once you know where to look.

He is in the way I talk. Half the slang I use came from him, words and phrases that started as his and slowly, without my noticing, became mine. He is in the music I listen to, songs he played in the car that I now play when I'm alone. He is in football, in every match I watch and every argument I start about it.

Sometimes I hear myself say something and realise it isn't my sentence at all. It's his. He just isn't here to say it anymore, so I say it for him.

## A part of life

> Death exists, not as the opposite but as a part of life.

When I first read it, it sounded like something you put on a poster. Now I think it is simply a description of what happened.

If death were the opposite of life, then he would have ended. A door would have closed, and everything behind it would have stopped. But that isn't what I feel. He didn't stop. He moved. He moved out of his own life and into ours. Into the way I speak, the songs on my phone, the team I shout at on weekends, and the calm I try to find when everything around me is loud.

His death isn't something outside my life, waiting at the edges. It sits right inside it, like a quiet room in a house I still live in. I walk past it every day. Some days I go in and sit for a while. Some days I just know it's there.

Death took him away, but it did not take away what he made of me. That part is still here, and it is still living.

## For him

There are things I never said to you, because between us they never needed saying. I'm saying them now.

Thank you for every fight, every laugh and every ridiculous night we shared. Thank you for sixteen years of showing up, without ever once being asked.

I think about what you would say if you read this. You would probably laugh, tell me I'm overthinking it, and change the subject. That would be fair. But I needed to write it anyway.

I'm talking about you now. I'm sorry it took me so long.
